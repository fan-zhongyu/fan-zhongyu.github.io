The life of a signal.

An interactive study of the gap between being seen, taking part, and moving on.

The work

A synthetic social field is driven by an aggregate model of potential participants, active participants, and circulating information. The same dynamics can be read through three lenses: a product launch, a public-health message, or an online rumor.

The field translates population-level quantities into 260 marks. Hollow circle: not reached. Solid circle: active. Cross: moved on. During comparison, the grey field shows a counterfactual rather than a second observed population.

Theory

Let P denote people not yet reached, A active participants, and I circulating information or attention. Their aggregate dynamics are:

Ṗ=−βPI

Ȧ=βPI − δA/(A + κ) − μA2 − ξA3

İ=ηA − νI

Attention helps convert an unreached population into active participation; active participation creates more attention. Drop-off and crowding eventually weaken that feedback, producing take-off, a peak, and a long tail.

When no new activity is feeding the signal, İ = −νI. It follows that I(t) = I0e−νt, with an attention half-life of t1/2 = ln(2)/ν. The half-life control changes ν directly.

Boundary

This is an explanatory experiment, not an empirical forecast. The people, communities, and links are synthetic; they make aggregate behaviour legible, but do not establish individual transmission paths or causal effects.

Possible extensions

  1. Replace the synthetic field with an agent-based network containing heterogeneous people, communities, and thresholds.
  2. Estimate parameters from real campaign, platform, public-health, or media time series.
  3. Run stochastic ensembles so that every trace carries uncertainty rather than a single deterministic future.
  4. Introduce competing narratives, interventions, geography, or platform moderation and compare who is reached—not only how many.
